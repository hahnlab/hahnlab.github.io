# Research

Our work focuses broadly on questions of **organismal function and evolution using genomic data**.  
We are particularly interested in the relative roles of **natural selection and genetic drift** in shaping nucleotide, gene family, and gene expression variation.  
Although much of our empirical work centers on humans, flies, mosquitoes, and wild tomatoes, lab members explore diverse systems.

---

## The Evolution of Gene Gain and Loss
- Developed **CAFE** (stochastic birth–death model for gene family evolution).  
- Identified large-scale patterns of gene gain/loss and their evolutionary implications.  
- Studied gene movement (esp. sex chromosomes) and copy-number variants under local selection.  

---

## Population Genomics
- Designed methods to distinguish **demography vs. selection**.  
- Applied clinal variation to uncover **local adaptation**.  
- Extended classical models to genomic-scale data.  

---

## Speciation Genomics
- Focused on *Anopheles gambiae* species complex.  
- Studied **introgression** and **selection** as drivers of heterogeneous genomic divergence.  
- Developed tools to detect introgression and distinguish it from locus-specific selection.  

---

## The Evolution of Transcriptional Regulation
- Investigated variation in transcription factor binding sites.  
- Leveraged RNA-seq to link gene expression divergence with evolutionary forces.  
- Current focus: wild tomatoes (*Solanum*) as models for functional genomic studies.
